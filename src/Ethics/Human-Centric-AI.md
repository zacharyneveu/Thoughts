# Human Centric AI

Recently, AI has been having a bit of a moment. In particular, a variety of small improvements in text-based generative models (known as Large Language Models or LLMs) have started to yield results which have crossed the threshold for being useful in a number of different tasks. This has enabled generative models to be used for new tasks such as generating marketing content/academic papers/code, and question answering similar to a next-generation search engine. The current capabilities have started to change the way certain jobs are done. The bulk of the conversation, however, has been around future capabilities. Projecting the improvement trends from the past two years to the next two years, it is easy to imagine this technology creating massive change. This hype has been very exciting for me as these types of machine learning models are something I've spent a lot of time working on. In both good and bad ways, the new capabilities of these models have sparked the imagination of investors, technologists, and the general public alike. Unfortunately, many of the takes I have seen focus too much on hypothetical sci-fi scenarios, glossing over the short term effects of this technology. When thinking about a new capability or consequence of AI, it is necessary to consider the answers to some very basic questions.

## When?
When will AI get here? Is it here already? The problem with a lot of answers to this question is that they ignore it and focus instead on the shinier question of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). The minds of Silicon Valley seem to be uniquely focused on The Singularity - when AI becomes so smart that human intelligence pales by comparison. My opinion is the singularity may look nothing like most folks expect. AI models are already outperforming humans in many areas
